# Crafty Design

## Brand goals

- Professional, but clearly artist/maker-led.
- The site should showcase artwork as seriously as it sells products.

## Logo

- The customer-facing brand is **Breezy's Creative Co.**
- `crafty` is the internal project/repository name and is not the public brand.
- The official Breezy's Creative Co. logo should drive the eventual visual
  language rather than an invented replacement logo.
- The current official logo uses:
  - a large black scissors monogram
  - detailed botanical vines and flowers
  - a small illustrated bird
  - black line-art styling
  - an elegant high-contrast serif wordmark
- The logo suggests an editorial, botanical, artist/maker-led visual direction
  rather than a generic craft-store aesthetic.
- A vector/SVG version of the logo is not currently available.
- The supplied PNG is sufficient for design reference, but a transparent
  high-resolution PNG or vector asset should be obtained before final production
  use if possible.

## Color

Current direction:

- Use a restrained neutral foundation so the artwork and products provide much
  of the site's color.
- Favor warm ivory, paper white, charcoal/near-black, and subtle natural neutrals
  as the base.
- Any accent color should support the artwork rather than compete with it.
- Final palette values remain TBD.

## Graphic language

Current direction:

- Contemporary maker's atelier / editorial art-studio feeling.
- Draw visual inspiration from the official logo's botanical linework,
  scissors, flowers, fine illustration, and handcrafted detail.
- Appropriate motifs may include:
  - botanical line illustrations
  - thin hand-drawn rules
  - subtle paper or print textures
  - cut-paper or editorial framing
  - restrained scissors/cutting motifs
  - elegant asymmetry
- Expressive details should frame the work, not overpower it.
- Avoid generic Cricut-craft, farmhouse, pastel-Etsy, corporate ecommerce, or
  overly dark luxury aesthetics.

## Photography

Current direction:

- Real work should be the primary visual focus.
- Product and portfolio photography may include thoughtfully staged props when
  they support the subject and do not distract from it.
- Portfolio presentation may use varied image proportions and editorial layouts
  rather than forcing all artwork into identical product cards.
- Shop presentation should remain more structured and consistent for usability.
- Existing photography is useful as design reference, but final publishing
  quality should be evaluated per image.

## Explicitly rejected directions

- A generic corporate ecommerce-template feel
- An excessively flat visual direction
- An excessively dark visual direction
- An invented replacement for the official Crafty logo
- Expressive graphic design that makes storefront usability unclear
- Treating "Crafty" as the customer-facing business name
- A loud rainbow/craft-store aesthetic
- A rustic farmhouse aesthetic
- A generic pastel Etsy-boutique aesthetic
- Visual styling that forces all artwork into one narrow aesthetic
